Three myths worth dropping this term
Misinformation spreads fast in group chats. Here is what widely accepted health guidance actually says.
MYTH — WHAT GETS REPEATED
FACT — WHAT GUIDANCE SAYS
MYTH 01“Bad period pain is just part of being a woman — you push through it.”
Mild cramping is common. Pain that regularly keeps you out of class, wakes you at night, or is not eased by usual measures is worth reviewing with a clinician — several manageable causes and treatment options exist.
MYTH 02“If nothing hurts or itches, there is no reason to think about sexual health.”
Some infections cause few or no noticeable symptoms, which is why screening, contraception advice and vaccination are offered to people who feel completely well. Feeling fine is not the same as being checked.
MYTH 03“Three hours of sleep in the week, then a long lie-in — it balances out.”
Weekend catch-up sleep does not fully undo an irregular week. Most adults function better with a fairly consistent schedule of roughly 7–9 hours, and steadier sleep often steadies mood and appetite too.
Correcting a myth is a starting point, not an assessment. If something keeps worrying you, ask a qualified healthcare professional.
