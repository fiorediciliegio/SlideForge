Getting seen is a process
you can prepare for
Most delays are about navigation, not nerve. Four steps get you to a useful appointment.
1
Register and pick the right doorRegister with the student health service early, before you need it. General clinic for physical symptoms, counselling for mood and stress, sexual health service for contraception and screening, wellbeing team for study adjustments.
2
Bring five thingsStudent ID and any insurance or registration details; your dated notes; a list of every medicine, contraceptive or supplement you take; relevant past conditions or allergies; and the date your last period started, if that applies.
3
Ask these four questionsWhat might be causing this? What are my options, including doing nothing for now? What should make me come back sooner? And can I have that in writing, or a sick note for my department if I need one?
4
Know how confidentiality generally worksHealth services normally keep your information private and separate from your academic record, and usually do not tell family or tutors without permission. Limited exceptions exist when someone is at serious risk.
You can bring a friend, ask for a different clinician, request an interpreter, or talk before any examination.
Who does what
Typically availableStudent health clinic · counselling and mental health · sexual and reproductive health · pharmacy · wellbeing and disability support · peer groups.
Out of hoursIf the clinic is closed and symptoms are severe or frightening, use the urgent or emergency route for your area rather than waiting.
Service names and opening times differ between campuses — check your own institution’s listings.
