Three levels of response,
not a pass-or-fail test
Most days sit at level 1. Level 2 means book an appointment rather than wait and worry. Level 3 means act now. Moving up a level is information, not failure.
One sign can have many explanations
These are examples, not diagnostic criteria. If you are unsure which level you are in, treat it as level 2 and ask.
LEVEL 1 · SUPPORTIVE Everyday care that is working
  • Fairly regular sleep and meals
  • Cramps eased by usual measures
  • Taking prescribed medicine as agreed
  • Attending routine or screening visits
  • Talking to someone when a week gets heavy
KEEP GOING — NO ACTION NEEDED
LEVEL 2 · DESERVES ATTENTION Patterns to get checked, not to fear
  • Periods that become clearly heavier, longer or unpredictable
  • Pain that repeatedly interrupts study or sleep
  • Low mood or anxiety lasting more than about two weeks
  • New discharge, itching, or burning when passing urine
  • A missed period when pregnancy is possible
  • A new lump or skin change you have not seen before
BOOK A ROUTINE APPOINTMENT
LEVEL 3 · URGENT Warning signs that need care now
  • Bleeding that soaks through protection every hour for two hours or more
  • Sudden, severe abdominal or pelvic pain
  • Fainting, confusion, or severe dizziness
  • High fever with pelvic pain or feeling very unwell
  • Chest pain or difficulty breathing
  • Thoughts of harming yourself
  • After a sexual assault — urgent, confidential care is available
