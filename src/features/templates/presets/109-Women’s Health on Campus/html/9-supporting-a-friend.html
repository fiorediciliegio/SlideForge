You are her friend, not her clinician
When someone tells you something personal about her health, the most useful thing you can offer is calm attention and a nudge toward real care — not an opinion about what it might be.
Start with one question: “Do you want me to just listen, or do you want help working out what to do?”
Listen firstLet her finish. Reflect back what you heard. “That sounds hard, and I’m glad you told me” does more than any advice. Silence is allowed.
Keep it privateHer health is hers to share. Don’t pass it to the group chat or a flatmate “who’d want to know”, and ask before involving anyone else.
Refer, don’t diagnoseSkip the guesses and the remedies. Offer something concrete: “Want me to look up the clinic hours, or walk over with you?”
If she may be in dangerIf she mentions harming herself or seems severely unwell, stay with her and help her reach urgent care. Safety comes before privacy.
Also fine to say: “I don’t know.” You are not responsible for managing another person’s health.
