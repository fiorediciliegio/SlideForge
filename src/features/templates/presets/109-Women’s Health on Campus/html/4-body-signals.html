Notice the pattern, not one bad day
Noticing is not diagnosing. You are collecting useful information to share with a clinician, not naming a condition.
Your own baseline is the reference point. Six everyday signals are worth a quick mental note — what changed, how long it lasted, and what made it better or worse.
ENERGYCompare this week with your usual, not with a classmate's. Tiredness that lingers after rest is worth mentioning.
PAINNote where it is, how strong it feels, how long it lasts, and what helps. Location plus timing tells a clinician a lot.
MOODCount days, not moments. A low afternoon is ordinary; several low weeks in a row is worth talking about.
SLEEPHow long it takes to fall asleep, how often you wake, and whether you feel rested — more useful than total hours alone.
APPETITE & DIGESTIONA clear change in hunger, or new discomfort after eating that keeps returning, is information worth keeping.
CYCLE PATTERNS (IF YOU MENSTRUATE)Log dates, flow and pain across a few months. One unusual month means less than a pattern that repeats.
Bring your notes to an appointment rather than searching for a diagnosis online.
