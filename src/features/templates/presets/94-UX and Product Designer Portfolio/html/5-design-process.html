05 — DESIGN PROCESS
From 34 screens to four honest steps.
Nine months, three rounds of testing. The breakthrough was not a new screen — it was splitting a single legal form into stages users could leave and return to without losing progress.
STEP 01
Audit
Mapped all 34 existing screens and tagged every field as legally required, useful, or legacy.
OUTPUT
Field inventory
9 legacy fields cut
STEP 02
Re-sequence
Grouped the flow by what a user physically needs at hand: memory, then phone, then documents.
OUTPUT
4-stage flow model
Save-and-resume spec
STEP 03
Prototype
Built three progress-disclosure variants and tested them against the same task with 8 users each.
OUTPUT
3 tested variants
Winner: stage cards
STEP 04
Rewrite
Rewrote 26 compliance strings with legal in a shared doc — approved in two sessions, not two months.
OUTPUT
Plain-language kit
Reusable copy rules
STEP 05
Ship & measure
Rolled out behind a flag at 10 → 50 → 100%, watching completion and support tickets at each gate.
OUTPUT
Staged release
Live KPI dashboard
DECISION LOG — THREE TRADE-OFFS I OWNED
Longer flow, fewer steps at once
We added one screen to remove the sense of an endless form. Perceived effort beat raw screen count in testing.
Documents last, not first
Moving ID upload to the end cost us a compliance debate, but let users invest before the hardest task.
Plain copy over legal copy
Legal kept the binding text in an expandable panel; the summary line above it is what users actually read.
