Ingredients & craft.

Three short shelves, three small production rituals, and a list of neighbours who keep the kitchen honest.
The Grain Shelf

01Stone-ground, named, dated.

  • Country-sponge flour, milled a valley away, last batch dated on every bag.
  • Cultured butter for laminations, churned on Thursdays and frozen in 2 kg blocks.
  • Two sea-salts — one for bread, one for finishing — kept in small wooden bowls.
  • A little raw honey from the rooftop; never heated, never blended.
SHELF · A
The Bean Corner

02Three beans, one rule.

  • A Brazilian base bean for body and sweetness — always present.
  • An Ethiopian bean for the morning bloom — citrus and flower.
  • An Indonesian or Honduran bean depending on the season — depth and smoke.
  • Roasted in 4 kg batches the day before, rested at least twelve hours before pulling.
CORNER · B
The Production Rituals

03The three things nobody skips.

  • Every bread gets its own shaped proof — no two trays are identical.
  • Every espresso shot is tasted before it leaves the bar — the second pull is thrown.
  • Every bake is dated and signed on the back of the wrapper in soft pencil.
  • Every flour sack is folded the same way the chef's mother taught.
RITUAL · C

No. 1 — Patience

Cold ferments go as long as they need to. The clock is a guide, not a boss.

No. 2 — Hands

Dough is folded and shaped by hand. Machines are welcome, but only when they can't be felt.

No. 3 — Names

Every supplier on the shelf is named on the back of the menu.
