STATIC GOES TO WORK!
Once you choose WHO GETS THE CHARGE, a prank turns into a proper tool.

JOB 1 · THE COPY MACHINE
Charge draws the picture — powder just follows.
A spinning drum is given a static charge in the exact shape of your page. Fine toner powder is pulled onto only the charged parts, rolls onto the paper, and heat melts it in place.
RULE USED: OPPOSITES ATTRACT
JOB 2 · THE PAINT SHOP
Paint that curves around corners by itself.
Car factories charge the paint droplets one way and the car body the other. The spray bends toward the metal, wraps the edges evenly, and far less paint drifts off into the air.
RULE USED: PULL BEATS GRAVITY
JOB 3 · THE AIR CLEANER
Smoke and dust get caught before they escape.
Dirty air passes a charged wire, so every speck of dust picks up a charge. Oppositely charged plates then grab the specks and hold them — and clean air carries on out.
RULE USED: CHARGE THEN CATCH
JOB 4 · IN YOUR KITCHEN
Cling film grips with zero glue at all.
Pulling the wrap off its roll is a rub — so the film leaves charged. Those charges tug on the bowl's rim and hold the sheet down tight, with nothing sticky involved.
RULE USED: SAME TRICK AS THE BALLOON
A zap you can aim is not a nuisance — it prints your homework and cleans the air you breathe.
