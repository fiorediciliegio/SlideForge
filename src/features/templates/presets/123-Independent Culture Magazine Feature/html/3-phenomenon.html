THE XEROX
KIDS.
Photocopier as press   ✕   bedroom as publishing house

Somewhere between the last independent bookshop and the first algorithmic feed, a generation went back to paper. Not out of nostalgia — out of refusal.

They call themselves nothing, mostly. But their zines pile up in the same bookshops, mailed in the same brown envelopes.

A phenomenon needs no manifesto to exist — only a photocopier, a stapler, and the certainty that not everything belongs on a screen.

Four months with them across five cities. A movement that refuses to be branded.
