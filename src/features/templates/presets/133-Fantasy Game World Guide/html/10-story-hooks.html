Four Questions This Atlas Cannot Answer
The First Hook · sealed since 611
Why was the Ninth Kiln bricked up from the outside?
The official chronicle records that the ninth sun-kiln failed inward and was sealed for safety. The masonry says otherwise: the seal was laid by masons standing in the open air, working in haste, and the Concord's own law was cut away from the lintel before they finished. Someone closed it against something, and the record of that year was rewritten by a hand the archive will not name.
Whoever opens it inherits both the eighth fire and the reason for the ninth's silence.
The retired surname has come back to the ledger.
A Mirrowgate runner carries a name the throne struck from the rolls in 731. The Ledger Houses have begun quietly reopening that account — and someone is paying them to close it again.
The river is not falling evenly.
Above Sabb the Ossir loses a hand-span a year. Below the weir it loses three. The arithmetic points to a draw nobody has surveyed, somewhere in the eleven leagues the throne no longer patrols.
Old Vaunt's cistern is still full.
A port stranded for four hundred years should be dry. One swimmer says it is not, and that the water beneath the dead harbour is sweet. Four powers would go to war over the survey alone.
The Invitation
The gate opens at dusk.
Bring a vessel and a name.
Eight kilns are still lit, one river is still falling, and nobody has yet walked the eleven unpatrolled leagues. Sarkhet does not need a hero — it needs somebody willing to be owed a favour.
