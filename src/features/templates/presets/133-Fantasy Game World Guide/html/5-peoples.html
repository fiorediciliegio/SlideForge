Chapter V · Field Notes on Peoples
Three Peoples,
Three Answers
to Thirst
Sarkhet's cultures are not defined by where they live but by what they promised in exchange for water. Each people keeps a different covenant, and each reads the other two as a form of dishonesty.
How They Stand to One Another
Ledger & Bowl — cold tolerance. The basin buys crossings it will not bless.
Bowl & Net — open contempt. One measures water, the other wastes it.
Net & Ledger — profitable friendship, renegotiated each monsoon.
"Ask a stranger which vessel he carries
and you have asked everything."
Plate III — costume and household custom recorded at Sabb, Vel Cinnabar, and Mirrowgate
The Ledger Houses
Keepers of the Written Debt
Clan-families who record every cup owed and repaid in clay tokens pressed at a household shrine. Marriage is a merger of ledgers; a burned ledger is worse than a death. They wear layered ochre linen with a burgundy hem for each generation kept solvent.
Hold sacred — the unbroken account
Will not do — take a gift without price
The Bowl-Sworn
Pilgrims of the Measured Draught
Ascetics who swear at fourteen to drink only what a shallow bowl holds, and to carry that bowl unshaded for life. They walk in single file so no follower drinks before the one ahead, and their word settles disputes no magistrate can.
Hold sacred — thirst borne in public
Will not do — drink behind a wall
The Net-Kin
Weavers of the Tidal Compact
Coastal households who hold that water cannot be owned, only borrowed at the tide's pleasure. Kinship is woven, not born: a knot tied into a shared net adopts an outsider outright. They tattoo scale patterns for each storm survived.
Hold sacred — the knot freely tied
Will not do — swear an oath in writing
