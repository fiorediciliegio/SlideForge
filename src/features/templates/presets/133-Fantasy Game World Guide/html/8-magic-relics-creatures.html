Chapter VIII · Heat, Objects, and Living Things
Magic Is Plumbing, Not Prayer
Heat can be moved, stored, and stolen — but never created, and never for free.
I · The Logic of Heat
Three laws every kiln obeys
First · Conservation. Warmth drawn into a working is taken from something nearby. A conjured flame leaves frost behind it.
Second · Vessels. No body can hold the draw. Heat must pass through fired clay, brass, or glass — every practitioner is also a potter.
Third · Debt. Heat borrowed and not repaid within a day accrues against the borrower's own warmth. Old practitioners are cold to the touch.
II · Relics of Record
Three objects the empire counts
1 · The Ashen Crown. Cracked at the Ninth's failure; whoever wears it must ration the Ossir in person.
2 · The Assessor's Bowl. Blown from Pale Sea glass. Water poured in it cannot be lied about.
3 · The Ossir Lantern. A surveyor's compass-lamp that points to the nearest living water, not north.
III · Living Things of Note
Three beasts a traveller will meet
The Sailback — a placid basin grazer whose back-fins bleed off heat; herders read the fin colour to forecast the day's temperature.
The Pale Crawler — eyeless salt scavenger of the crust. Harmless alone; it swarms where the crust is thin, which makes it the Salt Walk's best warning.
The Emberthroat — a bronze-feathered raptor that nests in vent shafts and carries live coals to crack open nests. Blamed for four kiln-town fires.
