01 · Policy issue & executive summary
The affordability gap has become a labour-market constraint
Demand for licensed care has grown 11% since 2021, while licensed capacity has fallen every year for four years.
Supply is contracting. 84 licensed providers closed since 2021, concentrated in the 12 ZIP codes already classified as care deserts.
Price exceeds the affordability benchmark. Households pay 19% of income for infant care against the 7% federal benchmark.
The workforce cannot be retained. Educator median pay is $15.40/hour against a $21.10 county living wage; annual turnover is 38%.
Executive summary
01Unmet demand of 7,200 slots is now the county’s binding constraint on labour-force participation, costing an estimated $182M in forgone household earnings each year.
02Demand-side vouchers alone reach families fastest but add no capacity, and risk price inflation in the 12 desert ZIP codes.
03A blended path — capacity grants plus a wage supplement, with targeted voucher expansion — scores highest on reach, equity and durability.
04Requested action: approve the blended option and $71M for FY2027, with rate authority delegated to the Office of Policy & Budget.
